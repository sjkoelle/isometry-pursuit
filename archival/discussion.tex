\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have shown that multitask basis pursuit can help select isometric submatrices from appropriately normalized wide matrices.
This approach - \isometrypursuit~ - is a convex alternative to greedy methods for selection of orthonormalized features from within a dictionary.
Isometry pursuit can be applied to diversification and geometrically-faithful coordinate estimation.
Our results exemplify these applications, but more can be done.

On the theoretical side, a critical unresolved question is the relation of the brute force and \isometrypursuit~ minimizers.
Essentially, there are two things that can go wrong.
First, \isometrypursuit~ may trim insufficiently for brute force search to be computationally feasible.
Second, the recovered support may not contain the true minimizer.
While we have ruled out the second problem in the case where an isometric solution does exist, in practice we often see greedy recovery defeat \isometrypursuit.
Characterization of support recovery here is somewhat different from existing analysis of greedy \citep{Mallat93-wi, Mallat, Pati-93, Tropp05-ml} and convex \citep{Chen2001-hh, Tropp06-sg,Chen2006TheoreticalRO} basis pursuit formulations.
Partly, this is because we use the $\ell_1$ minimizer to distinguish between $\ell_0$ optima rather than to identify them. 
Also, \isometrypursuit~ can generate non-unique solutions even when the design matrix satisfies general position, which is not true for the univariate formulation \citep{Osborne2000OnTL, DOSSAL2012117, Chrtien2011OnTG, Tibshirani2012TheLP, Ewald2017OnTD, Ali2018TheGL, Schneider2020-qt, Mishkin2022TheSP,Dupuis2019TheGO,Debarre2020OnTU,Everink2024TheGA}, but 

The principal algorithmic variant of interest is the combination of the multitask lasso \citep{ Hastie2015-qa} extension of our estimator with $D$ function selection within $\mathbb R^B$.
First, introducing a Lasso penalty may ameliorate \isometrypursuit's reliance on the second stage of coordinate selection to complete the estimation process.
Using the Lasso penalty, we can smoothly move between the multitask basis pursuit solution and more sparse solutions \citep{Mishkin2022-yf}.
Second, we often may want to select some orthonormal subset of dimension much less than $D$.
Finally, response matrices may be extended beyond the identity.

These extensions may faciliate an interesting set of applications diversification in recommendation \citep{Carbonell2017-gi, Wu2019-uk, Langchain} and retrieval \citep{Gao2023-cn, Pickett2024-ad, In2024-um, Weiss2024-xm, Vectara}.
Maximum cosine similarity used in retrieval corresponds to the first coefficient of the Lasso regularization path \citep{Koelle2022-ju}.
Greedy algorithms predominate in diversification \citep{Carbonell1998-ji, Barioni, Drosou, Qin2012-ok, KUNAVER2017154, Guo-shengbo, Abdool,Yu2016AGA,  Huang2024-wr, Pickett2024-ad, 5895106, Zhu2020-vb}, and convex reformulations have been studied to add speed and convergence to a global minima.
