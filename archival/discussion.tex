\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have shown that multitask basis pursuit can help select isometric submatrices from appropriately normalized wide matrices.
This approach - \isometrypursuit~ - is a convex alternative to greedy methods for selection of orthonormalized features from within a dictionary.
Isometry pursuit can be applied to diversification and geometrically-faithful coordinate estimation.
Our experiments exemplify these applications, but more can be done.

A principal drawback of \isometrypursuit~ is its reliance on the second stage of coordinate selection to complete the estimation process.
$D$-sparse selection is sometimes computationally infeasible using \brute~ (e.g. for Wine when $D=13$).
Moreover, we often may want to select some orthonormal subset of dimension much less than $D$.
This is a particularly attractive idea for diversification in recommendation \citep{Carbonell2017-gi, Wu2019-uk, Langchain} and retrieval \citep{Gao2023-cn, Pickett2024-ad, In2024-um, Weiss2024-xm, Vectara}.
An avenue for solving this issue is the multitask Lasso \citep{ Hastie2015-qa} extension of our estimator.
Using the Lasso penalty, we can smoothly move between the multitask basis pursuit solution and more sparse solutions \citep{Mishkin2022-yf}.
This is particularly relevant as maximum cosine similarity used in retrieval corresponds to the first coefficient of the Lasso regularization path \citep{Koelle2022-ju}.
However, the standard max-sum diversity formulation that seeks to identify datapoints that are as far apart as possible is substantially different from the notion of diversity selected for by \isometrypursuit~ \citep{Kuo1993-zy, Ghosh1996-id, Alfonso-Cevallos-Friedrich-Eisenbrand-and-Rico-Zenklusen2016-ai, Ashkan2015-jv}. 
More investigation is needed to compare against the substantial literature of greedy algorithms \citep{Carbonell1998-ji, Barioni, Drosou, Qin2012-ok, KUNAVER2017154, Guo-shengbo, Abdool,Yu2016AGA,  Huang2024-wr, Pickett2024-ad, 5895106, Zhu2020-vb}.

The comparison of greedy \citep{Mallat93-wi, Mallat, Pati-93, Tropp05-ml} and convex \citep{Chen2001-hh, Tropp06-sg,Chen2006TheoreticalRO} basis pursuit formulations has a rich history, and theoretical understanding of the behavior of this approximation is evolving.
For example, that the solution of a lasso problem can sometimes be a non-singleton set is well-known \citep{Osborne2000OnTL, DOSSAL2012117, Chrtien2011OnTG, Tibshirani2012TheLP, Ewald2017OnTD, Ali2018TheGL, Schneider2020-qt, Mishkin2022TheSP,Dupuis2019TheGO,Debarre2020OnTU,Everink2024TheGA}, but \isometrypursuit~ can generate non-unique solutions even when the design matrix satisfies general position condition.
Besides the question of conditions for uniqueness, some other theoretical question posed by \isometrypursuit~ are how well the minimizer of a convex loss approximates the singular value loss, and the relationship with curvature.
