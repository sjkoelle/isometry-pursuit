\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We study diversification with Iris and Wine \citep{misc_iris_53, misc_wine_109, scikit-learn} and coordinate selection with Ethanol \citep{Chmiela2018-at, Koelle2022-ju}.
For Iris and Wine, columns of $X$ represent observations and rows represent standardized features; we create replicates by randomly retaining half the columns.
For Ethanol, we project bond-torsion gradients onto estimated tangent spaces in ambient dimension $B=252$ to form the columns of $X$, and sample molecular configurations for replicates (see \citet{Koelle2022-ju} for preprocessing).
We also design synthetic dictionaries to exhibit the strengths of \isometrypursuit~and challenge greedy selection: we augment a planted identity block with randomly oriented orthonormal blocks of dimension $d_c<D$.
All columns have unit length, so initial single-column choices cannot distinguish the planted columns from the added columns, although the planted block provides a known optimum $\ell=D$.
Figure~\ref{fig:experiment-datasets} reports dataset dimensions and replicate counts.
We compare the forward and backward greedy initializations followed by swap search defined in Section~\ref{sec:sparse_convex_coordinate_selection}, each applied to the full dictionary or after restriction by \isometrypursuit.
For synthetic datasets, regret is $\mathcal R_{m,r}=\ell_{m,r}-D$ relative to the known optimum; for real datasets, we use the best observed optimizer on each replicate, $\ell_r^\star=\min_m\ell_{m,r}$, and define regret as $\mathcal R_{m,r}=\ell_{m,r}-\ell_r^\star$.

\isometrypursuit~improves accuracy and initialization time on the large-$D$ synthetic dictionaries, and sometimes improves selections on every dataset, making it useful alongside the other searches.
At $D=64$, both IP pipelines attain the optimum on all $875$ matrices, compared with $526/875$ for full-dictionary forward initialization and swaps and $799/800$ completed cases for backward initialization and swaps (Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic-ground-truth-regret}).
For $D=32,64$, IP uses about one-tenth of the aggregate backward-initialization time, while $75$ backward initializations reach the time limit (Figure~\ref{fig:complexity-runtime-early-stop}); these timings exclude swap refinement.
IP also improves on the baseline of \backwardgreedy~followed by \forwardbackward~on some replicates of every dataset: IP followed by the same searches wins on $4\%$ of replicates in each of Iris, Wine, and Ethanol, and on $4.9\%$ of paired synthetic runs (Figure~\ref{fig:real-search-regrets}).
The supplementary real-data benchmark also shows competitive IP initialization times (Figure~\ref{fig:optimizer_parameter_sweep}).
Since no pipeline wins uniformly, we can run multiple pipelines and retain the subset with the lowest observed loss.
The supplement reports support cardinalities (Figures~\ref{fig:synthetic-support-size} and~\ref{fig:support_cardinalities}) and sensitivity to optimizer settings (Section~\ref{sec:optimizer_sensitivity}).
